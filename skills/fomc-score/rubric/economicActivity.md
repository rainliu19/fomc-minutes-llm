# economic_activity

**Question:** Does the assessment of growth and spending support tighter or easier policy?

Strong, above-trend activity that risks overheating is hawkish (+). Weak or slowing activity is dovish (-).

## Where to look

Keywords: "GDP," "real activity," "consumer spending," "business investment," "housing," "manufacturing," "above trend," "below trend," "slowing," "recession," "soft landing," "resilient," "momentum," "demand."
Sections: Staff Review of the Economic Situation; Staff Economic Outlook; Participants' Views.

## Anchors

| Score | What the minutes look like |
|---|---|
| +1.00 | Activity well above potential and accelerating; overheating is a stated concern |
| +0.75 | Strong and broad growth; most see demand as exceeding supply |
| +0.50 | Solid growth above trend; many see this as supporting inflation pressure |
| +0.25 | Moderate, steady growth; a few note resilience |
| 0.00 | Near trend; mixed indicators |
| -0.25 | Growth moderating; some note softening in spending |
| -0.50 | Clear slowdown; many see growth below trend |
| -0.75 | Contraction in key sectors; most raise recession concern |
| -1.00 | Recession underway or imminent; participants stress urgency to support demand |

## Sub-signals

1. Staff GDP outlook versus trend or potential.
2. Consumer spending and household balance sheets.
3. Business investment and manufacturing.
4. Housing and interest-sensitive sectors (these show policy transmission).

## Pitfalls

- Distinguish activity from labor market and inflation. Score only the growth and demand discussion here.
- A slowdown that participants call "welcome" or "needed to reduce inflation" is still a slowdown. Score the policy implication: it reduces the case for further tightening, so it leans negative. But if participants say the slowdown is "not yet enough," pull toward 0.
- Staff outlook changes (revised up or down relative to the prior meeting) count as evidence if the minutes state them.
