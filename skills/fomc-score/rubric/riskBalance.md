# risk_balance

**Question:** Which risk do participants weigh most heavily: too much inflation or too much weakness?

Emphasis on upside inflation risks is hawkish (+). Emphasis on downside growth or employment risks is dovish (-).

## Where to look

Keywords: "risks," "balance of risks," "upside risks," "downside risks," "uncertainty," "two-sided," "risk management," "dual mandate," "tilted," "skewed," "asymmetric."
Sections: Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook; Committee Policy Actions.

## Anchors

| Score | What the minutes look like |
|---|---|
| +1.00 | Nearly all see risks heavily skewed to higher inflation, which they put well ahead of growth risks |
| +0.75 | Most see inflation risks as dominant; downside risks acknowledged but secondary |
| +0.50 | Many emphasize upside inflation risk; some cite downside risks |
| +0.25 | Slight tilt toward inflation risk |
| 0.00 | Risks described as roughly balanced, or participants split |
| -0.25 | Slight tilt toward growth and employment risk |
| -0.50 | Many emphasize downside risks; some still cite inflation |
| -0.75 | Most see growth or employment risks as dominant |
| -1.00 | Nearly all see severe downside risk and treat it as the primary concern |

## Pitfalls

- This dimension is about *weighting of risks*, not about the current level of inflation or growth. A meeting can have high inflation (`inflation` is +) yet growing downside concern (`risk_balance` is lower).
- "Uncertainty is elevated" alone is 0. Score only when a direction is given.
- Use the participants' summary language ("a majority," "many") and any explicit statement on whether risks are "weighted to the upside" or "to the downside."
- Watch for risk-management framing (e.g. "the cost of cutting too late is higher than too early"). That is a strong directional signal even without a data reading.
