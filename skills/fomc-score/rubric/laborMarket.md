# labor_market

**Question:** Does the labor market assessment support tighter or easier policy?

Tight and overheating is hawkish (+). Loosening or weakening is dovish (-).

## Where to look

Keywords: "labor market," "unemployment rate," "payroll," "job gains," "job openings," "vacancies," "wage growth," "participation," "layoffs," "labor demand," "labor supply," "tight," "cooling," "softening," "maximum employment."
Sections: Staff Review of the Economic Situation; Participants' Views.

## Anchors

| Score | What the minutes look like |
|---|---|
| +1.00 | Very tight: unemployment well below estimated natural rate, wages accelerating, participants worry about overheating |
| +0.75 | Tight and still tightening; wage pressure a major concern for most |
| +0.50 | Solid and tight; many see it as consistent with upward pressure on inflation |
| +0.25 | Strong but easing slightly; a few see residual tightness |
| 0.00 | Balanced; supply and demand roughly in line; participants split |
| -0.25 | Gradual cooling; some note emerging softness |
| -0.50 | Clear loosening; unemployment rising, hiring slowing; many see weakness as a concern |
| -0.75 | Significant deterioration; most worry about the employment mandate |
| -1.00 | Sharp deterioration, rapid layoffs; participants stress urgency to support employment |

## Sub-signals

1. Unemployment rate level and change.
2. Payroll growth and hiring.
3. Wage growth, and whether it is seen as consistent with 2 percent inflation.
4. Labor supply (participation, immigration) versus demand.

## Pitfalls

- "Cooling" can be good news if described as bringing the market back into balance. Score the policy implication, not the tone.
- Weak labor supply with tight conditions is still tight; do not score it as weakness.
- A rising unemployment rate caused by rising labor force participation is not necessarily dovish; read how participants interpret it.
